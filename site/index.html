What it does not do

It finds known classes of problems, not novel ones, and it does not replace a human penetration test. What is planned for each limit:

  • Novel problems no rule describes

    Opt-in AI mode: a local model proposes leads and the rules check them. E9 measures whether it finds more.

    In 1.9.0, opt-in

  • Business-logic and authorization flaws

    Authenticated preview scans with test accounts from your seed data. Later: an IDOR baseline.

    Planned

  • Zero-days

    It will not find them. Planned: an alert when something in your SBOM becomes a known vulnerability.

    Planned

  • A human penetration test

    It will not replace one. Use it before and between tests; a planned ship check gives one pass or fail.

    Stays a limit

  • Accuracy on real code

    Penthera Bench v1 and E1 measure it on code we did not write, published whatever the result.

    Pre-registered

Planned integrations, none shipping today: Base44, Lovable, Bolt and other AI app builders; Vercel, Netlify and Cloudflare Pages previews.

06 · Questions

Before you install.

What will it change on my machine?

Nothing without asking. The guided start detects your setup read-only, asks what to check, and offers the hook, the CI workflow and the rest one yes or no at a time. npx penthera init --dry-run shows what it would do and changes nothing. A fix is a diff you approve, and Penthera never commits or pushes. What the guided start does

Does it work offline, and what leaves my machine?

The CLI has no telemetry and no account. With --offline it contacts only the target you named, and every report lists the hosts it contacted (network use).

On this site, the check runs in your tab and never sends what you drop or paste. The page counts anonymous page views and load times with Vercel's cookieless counters on penthera.org. Its Content-Security-Policy allows those two counters and nothing else, so that is all it can send.

Is it safe to point at any site?

Point it only at sites you own or are authorized to test. The default scan sends only requests a browser would send. Payload testing (--deep, --fuzz, --all) is opt-in and asks for confirmation, and the guided start scans your running app only after you confirm the app is yours.

Scanning your own Vercel or Cloudflare site? Allow your IP in the firewall first, or scan a preview URL.

Is it free and open source?

Yes. Penthera is MIT-licensed and complete on one machine, with no account and no paid tier for any feature. If something paid comes later, such as a team server, it is added on top and never removes a local feature. That is decision 6 in the manifesto.

How accurate is it?

On the public benchmark: precision {{BENCH_P}} (95% CI {{BENCH_P_CI}}) and recall {{BENCH_R}} (95% CI {{BENCH_R_CI}}), from {{BENCH_TP}} true positives, {{BENCH_FP}} false positives and {{BENCH_FN}} misses in labs we built ourselves. During the benchmark it made {{BENCH_EGRESS}} requests to anything other than loopback.

The sample is small and the labs are ours, so these numbers catch regressions; they do not measure real-world accuracy. Measuring that on other people's code is experiment E1, pre-registered before any data is collected.

Every rule is readable: {{N_URL}} URL scan templates, {{N_SECRETS}} secret and AI-key patterns, {{N_CAPS}} dependency capabilities, {{N_AGENTS}} AI-tooling rules and {{N_RECIPES}} fix recipes, on {{DEPS_N}} ({{DEPS_LIST}}). Benchmark results

How is it different from paid tools?

Penthera runs on your machine, offline, and decides with readable rules that show their evidence at file:line. Many commercial tools work in the cloud, at registry or organization scale, and some add AI review; in places they go further than Penthera. Each comparison page says where the other tool is stronger and how to run the two together.

Does it help with NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act?

It turns what it finds into evidence and drafts you can check, not a certificate. For NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555): repeatable technical evidence for the testable measures in Article 21(2), and drafts of the 24-hour, 72-hour and one-month incident reports (NIS2 in Penthera). For the CRA (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), whose Article 14 reporting has applied since 11 September 2026: a timeline with due dates, a draft of every report and a CycloneDX AI-BOM (the CRA in Penthera).

An auditor can check a signed bundle of scan results with hashes offline, with penthera evidence verify <dir> (how evidence works). Penthera does not make you compliant and does not certify anything, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by the European Union.

Is Penthera built with AI?

Yes, and we say how. Of the commits up to v1.7.0, 91% were written with Claude through Claude Code, and each names the model in its commit message. The maintainer sets the direction, makes the design and security decisions, and reviews what is merged. Generated code passes the same gates as any other change: the test suite, CodeQL, OpenSSF Scorecard and an internal audit before every release. How Penthera is built

How is it researched and tested?

Penthera is a research project with a tool you can use today. Each feature goes method, evidence, tool. The methodology states the threat model and what each check can and cannot see. Experiments that decide what ships next are pre-registered before any data is collected, and the results are published either way. What holds up ships in the next release. What does not is dropped.

Detection rules ship inside reviewed releases as signed, versioned rule packs; nothing is downloaded while Penthera runs. The research plan lists the experiments.

How can I help or challenge it?

If you think a decision is wrong, open a discussion labeled "challenge": say which decision, what you would do instead and what evidence would settle it. Report bugs and false positives as issues, read CONTRIBUTING.md to contribute, or sponsor its maintenance.

08 · About

Why Penthera exists.

I build Penthera in the open, at night, alongside my PhD in AI and architecture. In 15 years I designed and built more than 100 apps for startups and governments, some used by millions of people. Security was always expected of me, so I collected the tricks and scripts I run before anything ships. Penthera is that sanity check, for everyone.

AI coding tools let far more people build. The security basics have to keep up, or apps leak data and run up credit-card bills. I believe technology does more good than harm when it is designed for people. Penthera uses research-backed methods and what I learned building agents: readable rules, free and MIT-licensed, to help builders ship safe apps.

Questions or ideas: GitHub Discussions. Bugs and false positives: issues. A vulnerability in Penthera itself: security.txt.
